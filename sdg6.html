01 / THE CHALLENGE

Water pollution is hard to solve when local evidence stays scattered.

Communities may notice unusual colour, floating waste, nearby discharge, or recurring pollution events. But observations are often shared informally, lack consistent details, and may not reach the people who can investigate.

◉

Fragmented observations

Reports are spread across messages, photos, and verbal complaints, making patterns difficult to track.

⌁

Limited access to testing

A visual observation cannot identify a contaminant. Communities need a clear path toward qualified sampling and laboratory analysis.

↗

Unclear response pathway

People may not know what information to record, whom to contact, or how to follow up responsibly.

Important distinction: AquaTrace does not diagnose water safety. Colour, smell, or appearance alone cannot confirm whether water is safe or identify a contaminant. Qualified testing is required.
02 / THE SOLUTION

A simple evidence-to-action pathway.

AquaTrace connects community observations with structured reporting, preliminary concern prioritisation, and a responsible referral pathway. It is designed as a digital coordination layer—not a replacement for water laboratories or public authorities.

How it works

1
Observe and report

A resident records the water source, approximate location, date, and visible concern without touching or sampling unknown substances.

2
Organise evidence

The platform stores a structured report and groups similar observations by location and concern type.

3
Prioritise for follow-up

A transparent rule-based flag highlights reports needing faster human review. It is not a water-quality score.

4
Refer and track

A community coordinator can share the report with relevant local authorities, water utilities, or accredited laboratories and record the response.

What makes it different?

✓
Structured community evidence

A consistent format makes reports easier to review and compare.

✓
Transparent priority rules

Flags are based on the reported event, not hidden AI claims or invented sensor readings.

✓
Designed for low-cost pilots

A web-first prototype can be tested with schools and community groups before adding verified sensor or laboratory integrations.

04 / RESPONSIBLE RESPONSE

What should happen after a concern is reported?

AquaTrace helps organise information; qualified people must verify the water condition and decide on interventions.

01 · COMMUNITY

Record safely

Keep a date, general location, source type, and a description from a safe public place. Do not enter water, approach discharge outlets, or handle unknown waste.

02 · VERIFICATION

Request qualified testing

A relevant authority, water utility, or accredited laboratory should decide sampling methods and test for appropriate physical, chemical, or microbiological parameters.

03 · FOLLOW-UP

Track corrective action

Record whether the concern was acknowledged, investigated, verified, and addressed. Publish only verified updates and protect reporters' personal information.

Implementation roadmap

PHASE 1

Validate the need

Interview residents, teachers, water professionals, and local organisations. Map the existing reporting process.

PHASE 2

Build the pilot

Add secure accounts, verified location handling, evidence upload, and an authorised coordinator dashboard.

PHASE 3

Verify with partners

Agree on a referral protocol with an appropriate authority, water utility, or accredited laboratory.

PHASE 4

Measure and improve

Measure report completeness, acknowledgement time, verified cases, and resolved cases; publish an honest pilot review.

06 / XYLEM CHALLENGE ALIGNMENT

How AquaTrace addresses the Water Quality challenge.

Challenge fit

  • Prevent: make recurring pollution patterns visible so communities and partners can act earlier.
  • Detect / monitor: structure observations and route them for qualified verification; no unvalidated sensor claims.
  • Reduce: support follow-up and track whether corrective action is taken.

What must be proven in a real pilot

  • Whether residents and community groups will use the reporting flow.
  • Whether reports contain enough useful information for partner review.
  • Whether a local organisation is willing and able to receive referrals.
  • Whether the system improves response time or follow-up compared with the existing process.
Current prototype limitations: This single-file demo uses browser storage only. It has no live water sensors, laboratory data, AI contaminant detection, geospatial backend, verified partners, or live authority integration. These are future development and validation steps, not existing capabilities.
07 / PROJECT SUMMARY

AquaTrace in one minute.

AquaTrace is a proposed community water-quality reporting and coordination platform. It addresses a practical gap between local observations of possible pollution and the qualified organisations that can investigate them. By collecting consistent reports, flagging urgent situations for human review, and tracking referrals and responses, AquaTrace aims to help communities make water concerns more visible and actionable. The prototype demonstrates the reporting workflow; a real pilot would require community validation, authorised partners, privacy safeguards, and laboratory-confirmed evidence.

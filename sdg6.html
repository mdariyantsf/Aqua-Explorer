01 / THE CHALLENGE

Water pollution is hard to solve when local evidence stays scattered.

Communities may notice unusual colour, floating waste, nearby discharge, or recurring pollution events. But observations are often shared informally, lack consistent details, and may not reach the people who can investigate.

◉

Fragmented observations

Reports are spread across messages, photos, and verbal complaints, making patterns difficult to track.

⌁

Limited access to testing

A visual observation cannot identify a contaminant. Communities need a path toward qualified sampling and laboratory analysis.

↗

Unclear response pathway

People may not know what information to record, whom to contact, or how to follow up responsibly.

Important: AquaTrace does not diagnose water safety. Colour, smell, or appearance alone cannot confirm whether water is safe or identify a contaminant. Qualified testing is required.
02 / THE SOLUTION

A practical evidence-to-action pathway.

AquaTrace connects community observations with structured reporting, preliminary concern prioritisation, educational intelligence tools, and a responsible referral pathway. It is a coordination layer—not a replacement for water laboratories or public authorities.

How it works

1
Observe and report

Record the water source, general location, date, and visible concern from a safe place.

2
Organise evidence

Store structured reports and group observations by location and concern type.

3
Analyse indicators

Use transparent screening rules and educational tools to understand potential concerns.

4
Refer and track

A community coordinator can share reports with relevant authorities, water utilities, or qualified laboratories.

What makes it different?

✓
Structured community evidence

A consistent format makes reports easier to review and compare.

✓
Transparent risk screening

Priority flags use disclosed rules, not invented sensor readings.

✓
Practical decision support

Interactive tools help users understand risks and explore water-saving options.

✓
Low-cost pilot pathway

The prototype can be tested with schools and community groups before adding verified integrations.

05 / GROUNDWATER INTELLIGENCE

Understand the hidden freshwater resource.

Groundwater is an important source of freshwater for many communities. AquaTrace introduces the key indicators and questions that a real groundwater-monitoring system should examine.

◉

Groundwater level

Water-table depth and seasonal changes can help explain groundwater availability. Reliable local well measurements are needed to assess actual conditions.

⌁

Groundwater quality

Potential concerns include naturally occurring arsenic, salinity, iron, and microbial contamination. Laboratory testing is needed to identify specific parameters.

↗

Recharge and use

Rainfall, land cover, soil, geology, pumping, and water demand can influence groundwater recharge and pressure on aquifers.

Groundwater investigation checklist

Use this checklist to plan a responsible local investigation. It does not measure groundwater.

1
Identify the source

Record whether the water comes from a shallow well, deep tube well, or another source.

2
Find verified measurements

Look for documented water-level readings and laboratory reports from responsible organisations.

3
Check seasonal patterns

Compare measurements from different seasons and years where reliable data exists.

4
Seek expert interpretation

Consult qualified water professionals before drawing conclusions or recommending interventions.

Why this matters in Bangladesh

Groundwater is an important water source in Bangladesh. Arsenic contamination is a recognised public-health concern in parts of the country, but its presence cannot be determined from appearance, location alone, or this website.

Do not assume a well is safe because water looks clear. Use qualified testing and follow advice from relevant public-health and water authorities.

Future integration: verified well records, groundwater-level datasets, hydrogeological maps, and laboratory-confirmed quality results.

06 / WATER QUALITY EXPLORER

Understand the indicators used in water testing.

Explore common water-quality parameters and why qualified testing matters. These descriptions are educational references—not measurements of any water source.

PHYSICAL

Turbidity

Measures how cloudy water is due to suspended particles. It can affect water treatment and may indicate a need for further investigation.

CHEMICAL

pH

Describes how acidic or alkaline water is. It is one parameter among many and cannot independently establish drinking-water safety.

MICROBIOLOGICAL

E. coli

Testing for E. coli is commonly used as an indicator of faecal contamination. Proper laboratory methods are required.

CHEMICAL

Arsenic

Arsenic can occur naturally in groundwater. Testing is important in areas where geological conditions may create exposure risks.

CHEMICAL

Salinity

Dissolved salts can affect freshwater usability, agriculture, and coastal water resources. Appropriate measurement is required.

CHEMICAL

Nitrate

Nitrate can enter water from agricultural and other sources. Concentrations should be assessed using appropriate analytical methods.

No results are displayed here because AquaTrace is not connected to a certified laboratory or validated water-quality sensor. Missing data must never be interpreted as evidence of clean or safe water.

How the calculation works

Conservation: households × daily use × reduction percentage.

Rainwater potential: roof area × rainfall in mm × collection efficiency. One millimetre of rainfall over one square metre equals approximately one litre before losses.

Rainwater potential is not a guarantee of usable or safe drinking water. Storage, first-flush systems, treatment, local rainfall patterns, and actual demand must be considered.
09 / RESPONSIBLE RESPONSE

What should happen after a concern is reported?

AquaTrace helps organise information; qualified people must verify the water condition and decide on interventions.

01 · COMMUNITY

Record safely

Keep a date, general location, source type, and description from a safe public place. Do not enter water or handle unknown waste.

02 · VERIFICATION

Request qualified testing

A relevant authority, water utility, or accredited laboratory should decide sampling methods and test appropriate parameters.

03 · FOLLOW-UP

Track corrective action

Record whether the concern was acknowledged, investigated, verified, and addressed. Publish only verified updates.

11 / SCIENTIFIC TRANSPARENCY

Evidence, methodology and limitations.

Scientific transparency is essential. AquaTrace distinguishes user observations, educational information, calculated estimates, and verified measurements.

USER-REPORTED

Community observations

Reports are statements entered by users. They are not independently verified and do not prove contamination.

RULE-BASED

Preliminary screening

Priority categories use disclosed rules to organise follow-up. They are not laboratory results or validated predictive risk scores.

CALCULATED

Scenario estimates

Simulator results are calculated from user-provided assumptions. They are not observed water savings or measured rainfall.

Reference sources for further research

These authoritative resources provide scientific and policy context. AquaTrace is not directly connected to their databases in this prototype.

  • World Health Organization — Drinking-water quality guidelines
  • WHO / UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme — Water, sanitation and hygiene data
  • United Nations — Sustainable Development Goal 6
  • UNESCO — World Water Development Report
  • FAO — AQUASTAT water resources database

Current prototype limitations

  • No live water-quality sensors or laboratory connection.
  • No verified groundwater-level database integration.
  • No official pollution incident database connection.
  • No real-time authority referral or notification system.
  • Local reports are stored only in the user's browser.
  • Risk screening is a transparent demonstration rule, not a validated scientific model.
  • Simulator outputs depend on user-provided assumptions and are not guaranteed outcomes.
12 / XYLEM CHALLENGE ALIGNMENT

How AquaTrace addresses the Water Quality challenge.

Challenge fit

  • Prevent: make recurring pollution patterns visible so communities and partners can act earlier.
  • Detect / monitor: structure observations and route them for qualified verification.
  • Reduce: support follow-up and explore practical water-conservation measures.
  • Educate: explain water-quality and groundwater indicators through transparent tools.

What must be proven in a real pilot

  • Whether residents and community groups will use the reporting flow.
  • Whether reports contain useful information for partner review.
  • Whether a local organisation can receive and review referrals.
  • Whether the system improves response time or follow-up.
  • Whether scenario estimates can be validated against real community data.
The prototype demonstrates a reporting workflow and educational decision-support tools. It does not yet demonstrate verified pollution reduction or measured improvements in water quality.
13 / PROJECT SUMMARY

AquaTrace in one minute.

AquaTrace is a community-first water intelligence and coordination prototype for SDG 6. It addresses the gap between local observations of possible water pollution and the qualified organisations that can investigate them. The platform demonstrates structured community reporting, transparent concern prioritisation, educational groundwater and water-quality information, and scenario-based water-conservation calculations. Its goal is to help communities organise evidence, understand potential concerns, and explore responsible solutions. A real pilot would require community validation, authorised partners, privacy safeguards, and laboratory-confirmed evidence.

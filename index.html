01 / UNDERSTANDING WATER

Water Quantity: How Much Water Does Earth Have?

Earth looks like a blue planet, but only a small share of its water is freshwater, and an even smaller share is easily accessible for human use.

The distribution of Earth's water

Most of Earth's water is found in oceans and seas. This saltwater cannot be used directly for most drinking and agricultural needs.

Most freshwater is stored in glaciers, ice caps and underground. Only a small fraction is found in rivers and lakes.

Key idea: Water exists in large quantities, but accessible freshwater is limited and unevenly distributed.

Why freshwater availability matters

  • Human drinking and sanitation
  • Agriculture and food production
  • Industry and energy production
  • Healthy rivers, wetlands and ecosystems
Availability depends on location, season, water quality, infrastructure and how water is managed.

02 / THE CHALLENGE

Understanding the Water Quantity Challenge

Water scarcity is not only about how much water exists. It is also about where water is available, when it is available and whether it can be safely used.

🌡️

Climate Change

Changing rainfall patterns, droughts and higher temperatures can affect water availability and increase pressure on water resources.

🏙️

Population Growth

Growing communities increase demand for drinking water, food production, sanitation and public services.

🚜

Overuse of Groundwater

Pumping groundwater faster than it is naturally replenished can lower water tables and affect long-term availability.

🏭

Water Pollution

Pollution can make rivers, lakes and groundwater unsuitable or more expensive to treat for human and ecosystem needs.

🌾

Agricultural Demand

Irrigation is essential for food production, but inefficient systems can waste water and increase pressure on local supplies.

⚖️

Unequal Distribution

Some regions have abundant water while others face seasonal or long-term shortages. Infrastructure and access also matter.

🌧️

Rainwater Harvesting

Collect and store rainwater where suitable, following local safety and water-quality guidance.

🌱

Efficient Irrigation

Improve irrigation planning and use suitable systems to reduce unnecessary water loss.

05 / LEARN MORE

Water Quantity — Frequently Asked Questions

Explore some important questions about Earth's water, freshwater availability and water scarcity.

1. How much water is there on Earth?

Earth contains an enormous amount of water, estimated at about 1.386 billion cubic kilometres. Most of it is saltwater in oceans and seas.

2. What percentage of Earth's water is freshwater?

Approximately 2.5% of Earth's water is freshwater. Most of this freshwater is stored in glaciers, ice caps and groundwater.

3. Why can't humans use all the freshwater?

Much freshwater is frozen in ice or stored deep underground. Some sources are difficult to access, and others may be polluted or far from where people need water.

4. What is water scarcity?

Water scarcity occurs when available water resources are insufficient to meet the needs of people, agriculture, industry or ecosystems in a particular place and time.

5. What is groundwater?

Groundwater is water stored beneath Earth's surface in the pores and cracks of soil and rocks. It is an important source for households, farms and communities.

6. Can groundwater run out?

Groundwater can be depleted locally when it is withdrawn faster than it is naturally replenished. The impact depends on geology, recharge and water use.

7. How does climate change affect water availability?

Climate change can alter rainfall patterns, intensify droughts and floods, change snow and ice storage, and affect the timing and reliability of water supplies.

8. Why is water not distributed equally?

Rainfall, climate, geography, rivers, geology and infrastructure differ across regions. As a result, water availability varies widely from one place to another.

9. How can communities reduce water waste?

Communities can repair leaks, improve irrigation efficiency, monitor water use, protect water sources and reuse appropriately treated wastewater.

10. What is the difference between water quantity and water quality?

Water quantity refers to how much water is available. Water quality refers to its physical, chemical and biological condition and whether it is suitable for a particular use.
